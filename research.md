# RockWatch: Rockfall Prediction and Early Warning for Open-Pit Mines

## Scope and Evidence Note

This brief addresses SIH25071, **AI-Based Rockfall Prediction and Early Warning System for Open-Pit Mines**. It covers peer-reviewed research, data repositories, public sensor-product documentation, and the SIH-linked 2025 IEEE prototype. It is a scoping review, not a safety certification or a substitute for a mine geotechnical engineer.

Important distinction: a *rockfall* is a local block/bench detachment and runout event. A *slope failure* may be much larger and normally has a slower deformation precursor. A *rockburst/seismic bump* is an underground high-stress phenomenon. The last is useful for ML methods and seismic processing, but it is not a label-equivalent replacement for open-pit rockfalls.

## Executive Conclusion

The mature, field-proven layer is continuous deformation **monitoring**, not black-box prediction: ground-based interferometric radar, continuous laser scanning, GNSS/prisms/inclinometers, and geotechnical interpretation. Commercial systems can repeatedly measure sub-millimetre displacement and issue real-time alarms. They are excellent at identifying movement and acceleration; they cannot, by themselves, guarantee an exact collapse time.

The research frontier is multimodal and geology-aware ML: 3-D point clouds/stratigraphy + deformation trends + weather + operational data. The strongest published open-pit rockfall-specific study found here uses synthetic simulations generated from real 3-D point clouds and evaluates VAE-derived features with nonlinear regressors. A 2025 AI-alert paper reports CNN-LSTM fusion on **simulated** multimodal data. Neither is evidence that an end-to-end AI model has been safety-validated across real mines.

**RockWatch should therefore be a calibrated, zone-level risk and response platform, not a “collapse at 14:32” predictor.** It should attach every alert to supporting evidence, model uncertainty, and a geotechnical response rule.

## What Has Already Been Achieved

| Maturity | Achievement | How It Is Achieved | What It Does Not Solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production | Continuous all-weather slope displacement monitoring | Ground-based interferometric radar (GB-InSAR/RAR/SAR) measures phase/displacement across a slope; software maps trends and alarms on movement/acceleration. IDS's current IBIS-FM EVO advertises sub-mm real-time movement up to 5 km with scans as fast as 20-45 s; GroundProbe's SSR range combines targeted and broad-area radars. | Radar line-of-sight, atmospheric effects, occlusion, and interpretation. It detects/forecasts deformation, not every detached small rock. |
| Production | Near-real-time rockfall/change detection | Repeat terrestrial laser scans are registered, differenced, clustered and displayed as 3-D change/velocity maps. Maptek Sentry is a practical example: continuous XR3 laser monitoring, displacement/velocity/inverse-velocity histories, alerts and 3-D imagery. | Costs, line of sight, dust/rain/vegetation, and scan cadence. Small fast falls can happen between scans. |
| Production | Subsurface and point instrumentation | Inclinometers, extensometers, TDR, piezometers, GNSS and prisms measure displacement, pore pressure, crack opening or water condition at known critical locations. | Sparse spatial coverage; installation/maintenance and a missed weak plane remain risks. |
| Production | Human-led TARP early warning | Geotechnical teams combine mine geometry, structural mapping, rainfall/blast history and deformation. They define trigger-action-response plans (TARPs): alert, clear zone, inspect, mine-off/support, reopen. Inverse-velocity extrapolation is a common decision aid. | Inverse velocity has case-dependent failure-time uncertainty; it should not be treated as ground truth. |
| Research/prototype | Vision detection and tracking | YOLO/CNN detectors find moving rocks or surface precursors in RGB/PTZ/UAV video; optical flow and photogrammetry quantify motion. | Very few real, labelled mine events; lighting, dust, rain, scale and camera shift damage generalisation. |
| Research/prototype | AI sensor fusion | CNNs encode imagery/point clouds, LSTM/temporal models encode sensor histories, and tree/ensemble models turn features into risk labels. | Synthetic or single-site performance must not be treated as an operational safety claim. |
| Research frontier | Geology-aware surrogate prediction | Stratigraphy inferred from 3-D point clouds is encoded with a VAE; XGBoost/RF/SVM/KNN predict simulated rockfall indicators. | Data available on request; labels generated by simulation. |

## Dataset and Data-Source Inventory

* **MRDataset (2026):** 3,921 real-world images; 12,314 labelled rockfall instances; YOLO-style boxes.
* **RockNet (2023):** 5.6 GB labelled seismic HDF5 data (rockfall/earthquake/non-event).
* **Assisi artificial-rockfall seismic test (2020):** Seismic recordings of controlled artificial rockfall.
* **Whitney Portal 2020 rockfalls (USGS):** TLS XYZRGB, UAV photos/GPS/SfM point cloud, runout boundary.
* **UCI Seismic Bumps:** Underground coal-mine rockburst/bump data for imbalanced classification.
* **ColoRadar:** 3-D FMCW radar, LiDAR, and IMU sequences.
