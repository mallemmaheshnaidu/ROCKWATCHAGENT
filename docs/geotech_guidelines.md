# Geotechnical Field Guidelines & Kinematic Stability

[GEO-GUIDE-01] Planar Sliding Kinematics:
Planar failure occurs when a daylighting geological discontinuity dips out of the slope face at an angle flatter than the bench slope angle but steeper than the internal friction angle of the joint surface. Critical daylight condition: Dip_joint < Slope_face AND Dip_joint > Friction_angle.

[GEO-GUIDE-02] Wedge Failure Criteria:
Wedge failure requires two intersecting discontinuity planes whose line of intersection daylights into the pit void. The plunge of the intersection line governs kinematics: plunge must exceed friction angle. Drainage and pore pressure reduction are primary stabilization countermeasures.

[GEO-GUIDE-03] Inverse Velocity Interpretation (Fukuzono Method):
The reciprocal of displacement velocity (1/v) plotted over time serves as an empirical precursor aid. In brittle rock mass failures, 1/v exhibits a linear decrease toward zero at the point of collapse. Limitation: ductile slopes, complex multi-bench failures, and sudden rainfall pore spikes can produce non-linear acceleration offsets.

[GEO-GUIDE-04] Blast Vibration and Dynamic Triggering:
Peak Particle Velocity (PPV) exceeding 50 mm/s measured at the bench crest induces tensile crack dilation. Following production blasts within 500m of an active face, a mandatory 60-minute sensor settlement and seismograph observation window must elapse prior to personnel re-entry.

[GEO-GUIDE-05] Ground Water and Pore Pressure Effects:
Pore water pressure acting within tension cracks reduces the effective normal stress on potential sliding surfaces. Heavy precipitation can trigger sudden detachments with minimal kinematic warning. Transient piezometer spikes must be treated with immediate precautionary exclusion.
